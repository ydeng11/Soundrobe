## Ownership design under review

The native supervisor must begin before the Node worker performs preparation, including source/input hashing and fixture setup. A separate broker process retains durable authority. The supervisor owns the driver/inline-fixture group and evaluator groups; a broker watchdog independently retains the registered group identities and monitors the supervisor. Neither watchdog may hold or acquire a journal lock to revoke in-memory admission or initiate termination.

Prefer native-owned spawning and a native startup gate over registering arbitrary Node-supplied PIDs after work starts. The gate must not permit effects until both owners have accepted the group identity. A group leader's PID, PGID, ownership and lifetime must be verified before signalling. No arbitrary PID control endpoint is permitted. Python/scoring descendants inherit their registered driver's group; detached evaluators and the broker require explicit native registration. Participants are trusted reviewed mock code, not a malicious-user sandbox.

Use one monotonic preparation deadline of 300 seconds, a once-only execution transition with a 600-second deadline, 250-ms heartbeats and a 1,000-ms lease. Subordinate timeouts clip to the remaining phase scope. Test-shortened durations must be explicit, source-bound and never represented as the production-proposed limits. No phase restart or extension is allowed.

At deadline or owner loss, latch admission closed before any journal attempt and request immediate termination of registered execution groups. Wait for actual group quiescence within the original ten-second failure grace. A termination request, leader exit, closed IPC stream or successful signal alone is not quiescence. No intentional readback/audit drain occurs after deadline. At most one bounded failure receipt (64 KiB) may publish after confirmed quiescence; otherwise retain incomplete membership/unknown effects and deny resume. Already-admitted operations can finish; no rollback, physical-I/O cancellation or billing cessation is promised.

Normal success needs a new handoff: Node performs audit/scoring inside the original execution scope, stages provisional success and exits without waiting for final authority publication. Native ownership verifies execution-group exit before authoritative publication. The current six-second broker drain and Node settle-before-exit cannot remain in this mode. Broker provisional completion cannot itself be a final success receipt. Deadline/stop must still win against late publication.

Rejected alternatives: Node timers cannot supervise a blocked event loop; a watchdog inside the evaluated process cannot survive that owner's loss; registering only leaders cannot prove descendant termination; killing by a stale bare PID is unsafe.
